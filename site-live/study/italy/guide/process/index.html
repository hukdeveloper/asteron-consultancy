Study in Italy — Step-by-Step Process

A clear path from checking eligibility to submitting your application, for the 2027/28 intake.

Why study in Italy

  • •Up to €8,200 annual stipend on regional scholarships
  • •No tuition fees at Italian public universities
  • •Many programs don't require IELTS
  • •Schengen visa access across the EU
  • •Globally ranked universities

  1. Step 1 — Check your eligibility

    Confirm your course actually exists in Italy, and that you meet its CGPA and English-language requirements.

    Bachelor's courses →Master's courses →University directory →

  2. Step 2 — Gather your documents

    Have everything ready before you start applying so you're not scrambling against a deadline.

    For Master's applicants
    • • Valid passport
    • • Bachelor's degree & transcript (or a Hope Certificate if still completing)
    • • High school records
    • • Two recommendation letters, if the program asks for them
    • • A motivation letter tailored to the program
    • • Proof of English proficiency — an MOI letter or IELTS
    • • A CV, ideally in Europass format
    • • Passport photo, plus job/internship letters and certifications if relevant
    For Bachelor's applicants
    • • Valid passport
    • • High school records (or a Bonafide Certificate if still pending)
    • • Two recommendation letters, if required
    • • A motivation letter
    • • IELTS — required by most Italian universities
    • • CV and passport photo
    • • TOLC entry test results — required by most universities

  3. Step 3 — Shortlist your programs

    Search the course guides for your field, and keep a simple table of course + university pairs you're interested in. There are only around 50–60 Bachelor's courses taught in English in Italy, so it's worth scanning the full list.

  4. Step 4 — Track admission dates & deadlines

    Cross-check your shortlist against each university's expected admission window and deadline in our directory, and prioritise whichever closes soonest.

    University directory →

  5. Step 5 — Apply directly

    Apply straight through each university's own portal — our directory links to the official application page for every university, not to a third-party site. Requirements can differ program to program even within the same university, so read each listing carefully.

Once you're admitted, most universities and regions require translated, legalized documents for enrolment and scholarships — see Regional Scholarships and Italian Translation, and check Insurance requirements before your visa appointment.
